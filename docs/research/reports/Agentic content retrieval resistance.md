# Route around the walls, don't climb them

By late October 2026, the HTML of nearly every high-value research site refuses an honest automated client. In our probe on 2026-10-31, a plain, honestly identified client got usable page content from only about 10 of roughly 35 targets. About 17 answered 403 or 999 bot-protection pages, and the rest answered HTTP 200 with a JS shell or login wall (our probe, 2026-10-31). Much of the same content is still reachable through sanctioned side doors that worked keyless in the same run: the Stack Exchange API, GitHub REST, HN Algolia, Medium RSS, Reddit RSS (until **2026-11-13**), Telegram `t.me/s/`, YouTube oEmbed, and the open scholarly APIs (arXiv, OpenAlex, Crossref, Unpaywall, Semantic Scholar, Europe PMC). search-cli's extract chain is built for the wrong problem. Its first rung impersonates Chrome 136, which is stale, and forges a Google Referer. That is the conduct a July 2026 SDNY ruling (*Reddit v. SerpApi*) treated as plausible DMCA §1201 circumvention, and the tool-author "trafficking" theory from that ruling reaches the maintainers of open-source code. Meanwhile, its `scrape_rejection` check only recognises Cloudflare phrases, so a 731-character Instagram "Log In / Sign Up" shell counts as a successful extraction and stops the chain. The recommended direction has three parts: broaden content validation, add domain-routed adapters for sanctioned endpoints and a DOI pipeline, adopt an honest identity and robots posture, and demote the stealth rung to an explicit opt-in. Two caveats bound the evidence. The impersonation rung and keyless Jina were **not tested empirically**: the agent's safety policy refused the impersonation probe, and no provider keys were configured. Many 2026 claims also come from scraper vendors and are flagged where used.

## Seventeen of thirty-five sites shut the door on an honest client

The empirical baseline comes from one residential IP on 2026-10-31. The client was `curl/8.9.1` with no impersonation, one GET per URL, and each result was classified by status, visible-text length and challenge markers (our probe, 2026-10-31). Separate live probes run by the topic researchers on the same day, some with a browser User-Agent, confirmed the vendor attribution. NYT answered 403 with `server: DataDome` and `x-datadome-riskscore: 0.587`. WSJ answered **401** with `x-datadome: protected`. Bloomberg set PerimeterX's `_pxhd` cookie. FT and The Economist answered 403 with `cf-mitigated: challenge`, and so did stackoverflow.com even when sent a Chrome UA. ScienceDirect, Wiley, ACM, T&F, SSRN, ResearchGate and Academia.edu sit behind Cloudflare challenges. IEEE Xplore answers 202 with an AWS WAF challenge (`x-amzn-waf-action: challenge`), and Springer and JSTOR serve a 200 "Client Challenge" interstitial ([ScienceDirect probe](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0140673620301835); [IEEE probe](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9000000); [JSTOR probe](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1885099)). Springer returned 181 KB of real text to the curl client but a challenge to the Firefox-UA probe. **Protection varies by request shape and moment, so it has to be detected by content, not assumed by domain.**

Robots.txt has moved from naming training crawlers to naming *user-triggered* agents. ScienceDirect's file, "Last updated: October 2026", is default-deny. It allowlists OAI-SearchBot and Claude-SearchBot for citations and explicitly disallows ChatGPT-User, Claude-User, Perplexity-User and **`claude-code`** ([ScienceDirect robots.txt](https://www.sciencedirect.com/robots.txt)). NYT and Bloomberg block Claude-User, ChatGPT-User and Perplexity-User. Stack Overflow serves non-browsers a robots.txt with HTTP **418**, `Content-signal: search=no, ai-train=no` and `Disallow: /`, plus an RSL licence pointer to CC BY-SA. Publishers want AI search to cite them, but they do not want agents to read them.

Status codes are an unreliable signal in both directions. Instagram (731 chars), Facebook (52), Threads (37), Discord (49), x.com (2 KB of "Log in / Sign up"), reddit.com (6 chars) and the YouTube watch page (252 chars, title only) all return **200** with no content (our probe, 2026-10-31). YouTube's caption `baseUrl` returns 200 with **0 bytes** unless a BotGuard proof-of-origin (PO) token is attached, which one vendor dates to April 2026 ([yt-dlp PO Token Guide](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/PO-Token-Guide); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/jamhimself/why-your-youtube-transcript-scraper-started-returning-empty-strings-and-how-to-fix-it-in-2026-20ed)). An independent-ish September 2026 benchmark of 16 paid unblocker APIs found that **16.6% of failures were "false successes"**, meaning a 200 status without the real page. The field averaged only 66.6% on bot-protected sites ([String benchmark](https://usestring.ai/web-scraping-statistics), run by a vendor that ranked itself first).

## Sanctioned side doors cover most of the research surface

The pattern across the probe is consistent. When a site walls its HTML, it often still publishes a machine interface, and that interface is both more reliable and legally cleaner. **Stack Exchange API v2.3** answered keyless with `quota_max: 300` per day per IP. A free stackapps key raises that to 10,000, and batching 100 IDs per call stretches either budget a long way ([api-evangelist rate-limit spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/api-evangelist/stackexchange/refs/heads/main/rate-limits/stackexchange-rate-limits.yml)). **GitHub REST** allows 60 requests/hour unauthenticated and 5,000 with a token. Code search returns 401 without auth, and raw.githubusercontent.com cannot be lifted by a token ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/using-the-rest-api/rate-limits-for-the-rest-api); [GitHub Changelog 2025-05-08](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-05-08-updated-rate-limits-for-unauthenticated-requests/)). **HN's Algolia and Firebase APIs** need no key. **Medium's RSS** returned 153 KB with full post bodies even though the publication page was Cloudflare-blocked (our probe, 2026-10-31). That settles a gap the social-platform research left open. **Substack's** `/feed` carries about 20 recent posts, and the undocumented `/api/v1/posts` reaches further back for free posts ([thedatacollector](https://thedatacollector.substack.com/p/how-to-scrape-substack-newsletters)). **Telegram's** `t.me/s/<channel>` preview is anonymous and paginates with `?before=` ([DEV: sndzdata](https://dev.to/sndzdata/how-to-scrape-a-public-telegram-channel-without-an-account-or-api-key-542j)).

Scholarly content has the most mature open layer, and it is getting metered. In live probes, Crossref returned an abstract and a licence, Unpaywall returned `best_oa_location.url_for_pdf`, Semantic Scholar returned `openAccessPdf` and external IDs, and Europe PMC returned **177 KB of JATS full text** for an open-access paper. arXiv, bioRxiv, DOAJ and OpenAlex all answered keylessly ([Crossref probe](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1038/s41586-021-03819-2); [Europe PMC fullTextXML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8371605/fullTextXML)). The metering is tightening in several places. Crossref's first rate-limit change since 2013 took effect 2025-12-01: the public pool allows 5 single-record req/s with concurrency 1, and the `mailto=` polite pool allows 10 with concurrency 3 ([Crossref blog](https://crossref.org/blog/announcing-changes-to-rest-api-rate-limits/)). **Unpaywall returns HTTP 422 without an `email` parameter.** OpenAlex now runs a dollar budget of about **$0.10/day keyless** and $1/day with a free key; singleton lookups cost $0, but cursor paging needs a key ([OpenAlex docs](https://help.openalex.org/how-to-use-the-api/rate-limits-and-authentication)). Semantic Scholar's shared keyless pool returned 429 to a search request moments after a DOI lookup succeeded, and CORE's keyless allowance was exhausted at 10 ([S2 API](https://www.semanticscholar.org/product/api)). A DOI pipeline therefore needs a configurable contact email and optional keys from day one. arXiv's terms cap clients at one request every 3 seconds across all of a user's machines ([arXiv API ToU](https://info.arxiv.org/help/api/tou.html)).

News and YouTube are where the side doors narrow to metadata. NYT RSS and Google News RSS give headline, link, date and a short description. The NYT Article Search API returns abstract and lead paragraph with a free key, and quotas conflict across secondary sources. YouTube's only official caption download requires edit permission on the video ([YouTube Data API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/captions/download)). The practical route is to shell out to yt-dlp with the **bgutil PO-token provider**, which a yt-dlp maintainer maintains. The yt-dlp guide (edited 2026-07-12) says the `web` client needs PO tokens for subtitles ([yt-dlp wiki](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/PO-Token-Guide)). That route is honest about its own fragility but not risk-free: it generates the token the BotGuard check expects, so it sits in the same conceptual neighbourhood as impersonation. Keep it opt-in and dependent on an external install.

Archives are a thinner safety net than they were. Google's cache is gone ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-cache-dead-38112.html)). Reddit limited the Wayback Machine to its homepage in August 2025 ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/reading/reddit-will-block-the-internet-archive/)). NYT, FT, USA Today and more than 340 local outlets have blocked the Internet Archive crawler since late 2025 ([Nieman Lab 2026-01](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/01/news-publishers-limit-internet-archive-access-due-to-ai-scraping-concerns/); [WGCU 2026-05-22](https://www.wgcu.org/section/science/2026-05-22/over-340-local-news-outlets-are-limiting-the-internet-archives-access-to-their-journalism)). The Wayback availability API answered **429 on the very first request** from our IP, twice, and CDX timed out (our probe, 2026-10-31). Community reports put the informal ceiling around 15 req/min/IP ([Ciro Santilli](https://cirosantilli.com/wayback-machine-rate-limit)). Wayback is worth a paced, single-shot fallback for static and older pages, with 429 treated as terminal for the run. archive.today has no API, faces an FBI subpoena (October 2025) and is reportedly blacklisted by English Wikipedia ([giga.law](https://giga.law/daily-news/2025/11/6/fbi-subpoena-seeks-information-on-registrant-of-archive-domains)). Its main use is paywall bypass, and a tool that defaults to it inherits that framing.

## The stealth rung buys little coverage at a growing legal price

The extract chain in `src/engine.rs` (`execute_special`) runs `stealth → jina → firecrawl → browserless`. The local rung in `src/providers/stealth.rs` does more than a faithful browser fetch would. It builds a wreq client with `Emulation::Chrome136`, hard-codes `Sec-Ch-Ua` for Chrome 136 on macOS (the probe host is Linux), and injects a forged `Referer: https://www.google.com/search?q=<domain>` that the code comments label a "Scrapling technique". It is a default Cargo feature (`default = ["stealth"]`), although the Cargo comment notes that wreq's BoringSSL does not link on Linux, so many Linux builds silently ship without it. Everything after the stealth rung fetches with an honest `search-cli/<version>` User-Agent from `src/context.rs`, which carries no contact URL.

**Effectiveness is weak and decaying.** Vendors score JA3/JA4 TLS, HTTP/2 framing, header shape, IP reputation and injected-JS sensor data together. A client that matches Chrome's TLS but not its other signals reads as a "wrong-shape Chrome" ([ProxyLabs](https://proxylabs.app/blog/ja4-tls-fingerprinting-2026); [krowdev](https://krowdev.pages.dev/article/bot-detection-2026.md)). curl_cffi's own README says TLS fingerprinting alone is not enough ([curl_cffi](https://github.com/lexiforest/curl_cffi)). A vendor Q&A says JS-less impersonation "tends to work" only against Cloudflare without Turnstile, DataDome XHR endpoints and medium-strength Akamai, and that an outdated profile is itself suspicious ([Scrappey](https://scrappey.com/qa/web-scraping-apis/what-is-curl-cffi), vendor claim). The wreq-util profiles run to Chrome 137, and a third-party listing claims newer releases reach Chrome 147 ([wreq-util](https://docs.rs/wreq-util)). By late 2026, Chrome 136 is a stale profile. A macOS client hint arriving over a Linux network stack with a templated Google referer adds further inconsistency. No 2026 benchmark measures wreq specifically, and **our own impersonation probe was not run**, so the rung's real hit rate on this IP is unknown. The best available evidence comes from paid services with residential proxies and JS execution, and those averaged 65.6% on Cloudflare, 65.0% on Akamai and 57.6% on DataDome ([String](https://usestring.ai/web-scraping-statistics)). A bare TLS impersonator should do materially worse.

**Legal exposure rose sharply in July 2026.** In *Reddit v. SerpApi*, Judge Engelmayer (S.D.N.Y., 2026-07-31) denied dismissal of the §1201(a)(1)(A) circumvention and §1201(a)(2) trafficking claims. He held that a bot-detection system that admits humans but blocks bots plausibly "effectively controls access" even though the content is publicly viewable. He also treated Perplexity, which merely set parameters and ran queries, as a plausible *direct* circumventor. The tool at issue allegedly used proxies and "fake user-agent strings" ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/reddit-v-serpapi-llc)). Eleven days earlier, the N.D. Cal. *Google v. SerpApi* court had dismissed Google's §1201 claim, with prejudice where SearchGuard guards uncopyrighted results, because Google had not alleged the measure operated "with the authority of the copyright owner" ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/court-dismisses-googles-dmca-claims-against-serpapi/583033/)). The steelman for keeping the rung rests on that ruling: neither decision is appellate, the two conflict, and no court has applied §1201 to a general-purpose fetch library. The counterweight is that search-cli's targets are articles, posts and papers, all copyrighted content whose owners plausibly authorise the bot wall. That is the fact pattern where Reddit's theory is strongest. The trafficking prong attaches to whoever ships the capability, and open-source distribution does not obviously escape it. In the Reddit case, the more aggravating facts are the forged referer and spoofed client hints, the kind of "disguised identity after being blocked" that Cloudflare publicly sanctioned Perplexity for in August 2025 ([Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/perplexity-is-using-stealth-undeclared-crawlers-to-evade-website-no-crawl-directives/)). The weakness of the rung matters here too: a rung that rarely clears serious bot management delivers little in exchange for the exposure it creates. The agent safety policy that refused to run our impersonation probe on bot-check-circumvention grounds is an independent signal of how the conduct reads from outside.

**The validation gap matters more than the impersonation.** `scrape_rejection` in `src/engine.rs` flags two cases: binary garbage, and content under 4,000 characters containing one of six Cloudflare phrases. `reject_bad_scrape` inspects only the first result's snippet. The stealth rung does fail closed on non-2xx and on empty extracted text, so 403s and the 6-character reddit.com shell already escalate. The failure is on 200 responses carrying a short wall. Instagram's 731-character "Log In / Sign Up", X's 2 KB login prompt, YouTube's 252-character title page, JSTOR's "Client Challenge", and Reddit's 200-status "blocked by network security" page reported by vendors all pass as success and stop the chain. The marker list also lacks DataDome (`captcha-delivery.com`), PerimeterX (`px-captcha`, "Press & Hold"), Akamai "Access Denied" and AWS WAF. The same check runs on Jina and Firecrawl output, so a hosted provider that faithfully renders a login wall is also accepted as success.

**Jina and Firecrawl behave differently than the chain assumes.** Jina states that Reader "does not actively circumvent or bypass any website defense mechanisms", and that paid keys "do not grant access to additional websites" ([Jina Reader](https://jina.ai/reader/)). Its value is JS rendering and markdown cleanup, not unblocking. It offers an `X-Robots-Txt` header, which search-cli could send to delegate robots compliance. Keyless Reader exists at 20 RPM per IP, but `src/providers/jina.rs` returns `AuthMissing` without a key, so the "keyless Jina" rung does not exist in the shipped code, and it was not probed either. Firecrawl's `proxy` defaults to `auto`, which tries basic and then enhanced at 5 credits only when needed. A claim that the 5-credit charge ended on 2026-08-17 is unverified ([Firecrawl docs](https://docs.firecrawl.dev/features/stealth-mode); [Proxidize](https://proxidize.com/blog/firecrawl-pricing/)). search-cli's request body omits `proxy`, so it already gets `auto` implicitly. Setting it explicitly is hygiene against a default change, not new capability. Firecrawl returns "This website is no longer supported" for Instagram, YouTube, TikTok and other social platforms ([ScrapeCreators](https://scrapecreators.com/blog/firecrawl-social-scraping-restrictions), competitor source). Browserless's `/content` call sets no CAPTCHA-solving or proxy options, which keeps it on the defensible side of the line. Browserless bills CAPTCHA solves at 10 units per attempt, failures included ([Browserless docs](https://docs.browserless.io/overview/unit-consumption)). For walled social sites, adding more fallbacks will not help; only routing by domain to sanctioned endpoints will.

## Ten changes, ordered by return on risk

The list runs from highest recommendation down. Effort is S (a day or less), M (a few days) or L (a week or more).

1. **Broaden content validation.** *Effort S. Benefit high. Risk none.* Extend `scrape_rejection` with markers for DataDome (`captcha-delivery.com`), PerimeterX (`px-captcha`, "Press & Hold", "Are you a robot"), Akamai ("Access Denied", "Reference #"), AWS WAF, "Client Challenge", Reddit's "blocked by network security", and login-wall phrases ("Log in", "Sign up", "Sign in to continue") when the text is short. Add a minimum-content floor, for example under ~500 visible characters with no paragraph structure. Where the response is available, classify on headers before parsing: `cf-mitigated: challenge`, `x-datadome`, `_px*` cookies, `x-amzn-waf-action`, HTTP 999. Return distinct codes (`bot_wall`, `login_wall`, `js_shell`) so agents can tell "blocked" apart from "empty". Check every result, not just the first. This fixes the 200-status false-success gap that affects all four rungs.

2. **Add domain-routed adapters for sanctioned endpoints ahead of the generic chain.** *Effort M overall, S per adapter. Benefit very high. Risk low.* Before a URL enters the extract chain, rewrite it to the sanctioned endpoint by domain. stackoverflow.com and the other SE sites go to API v2.3 with `filter=withbody`, an optional stackapps key, and CC BY-SA attribution preserved. github.com goes to REST, using the contents API with `GITHUB_TOKEN` or `gh auth token`. news.ycombinator.com goes to Algolia `items/{id}`. Medium goes to `/feed/@user` or `/feed/<pub>`, matching the post by URL. Substack goes to canonical HTML or `/feed`, then `/api/v1/posts` for older items. t.me goes to `t.me/s/`. youtube.com goes to oEmbed metadata plus `yt-dlp --write-subs --write-auto-subs --skip-download` when yt-dlp is on PATH, with honest error codes for PO-token and `IpBlocked` failures. Reddit is time-boxed. RSS works until **2026-11-13**. After that, use OAuth only for users who hold an approved app, with UA `platform:app-id:version (by /u/name)` and 100 QPM honoured. New access requests close **2026-10-31**, unregistered apps lose access **2027-01-12**, and everyone without a written agreement loses it in **March 2027**. Ship the Reddit adapter so it degrades to "unavailable, here is the link" on those dates rather than falling through to stealth. Domains with no sanctioned route (LinkedIn, Quora, Glassdoor, Discord, Instagram, Facebook) should short-circuit to a clear `no_sanctioned_route` result.

3. **Build a DOI and scholarly pipeline with a configurable contact email.** *Effort M. Benefit very high for research users. Risk low.* Resolve a DOI from the URL pattern or a Crossref `query.bibliographic` search, and never fetch the walled landing page. Then call Crossref with `mailto` for metadata and licence, OpenAlex for abstract, OA locations and citations, Unpaywall with `email` for `best_oa_location`, and Semantic Scholar for `openAccessPdf`. Full text comes from Europe PMC `fullTextXML` or NCBI BioC for PMCIDs, arXiv HTML for arXiv IDs (throttled to one request per 3 s globally), and the bioRxiv API for preprints. Config needs `contact_email` (required for Unpaywall, which returns 422 without it) and optional `OPENALEX_API_KEY`, `S2_API_KEY`, `NCBI_API_KEY` and `CORE_API_KEY`. Surface `x-ratelimit-*` and `Retry-After`. When the OA copy sits on a Cloudflare-walled publisher host, prefer `host_type=repository` locations, or return metadata, abstract and link. This also replaces any Google Scholar dependency; Scholar's robots.txt disallows `/scholar`, and Google is litigating against SerpApi.

4. **Adopt an honest identity and robots.txt posture.** *Effort S. Benefit medium, since it unlocks Wikipedia-style "identify yourself" sites and builds goodwill. Risk reduces exposure.* Change the UA to `search-cli/<ver> (+https://github.com/<repo>; <contact>)`. Honour robots.txt by default for any multi-page, background or automated crawl. For a single URL the user explicitly supplied, fetch as OpenAI's ChatGPT-User does ("robots.txt rules may not apply" to user-initiated actions, per [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)), but never retry under a different identity after a block. Offer a `--respect-robots` flag that defaults to on, and pass Jina `X-Robots-Txt` so hosted fetches follow the same policy. Expect "no" from NYT, Bloomberg, The Economist, ScienceDirect and IEEE, which name user-triggered agents.

5. **Demote the stealth rung to an explicit opt-in and remove the disguise elements.** *Effort S. Benefit is legal and reputational. Coverage cost is small and unmeasured.* At minimum, drop the forged Google `Referer` and the hard-coded macOS client hints, take `stealth` out of `default` features, and require `--allow-impersonation` or a config key with a printed warning. The stronger option is to remove it. The case for removal rests on maintainer exposure under the §1201(a)(2) trafficking theory in *Reddit v. SerpApi*, set against weak measured value. The case for keeping an opt-in rests on *Google v. SerpApi* and the lack of any appellate or library-level ruling. If it stays, update the profile (wreq-util offers Chrome 137 or newer), because a stale fingerprint is a detection signal. That update should not happen before the validation fix in option 1, which makes the rung's real yield measurable for the first time.

6. **Add a Wayback fallback with pacing.** *Effort S. Benefit medium for static or older pages, nil for Reddit and blocking news outlets. Risk low.* Call `archive.org/wayback/available?url=` once, then fetch the raw `id_` snapshot, with a global limit of about 1 req/s and 429 treated as terminal for the run. Our IP got 429 on the first call. Label the output as an archived copy with its timestamp. Skip archive.today.

7. **Make Firecrawl's `proxy: "auto"` explicit and expose `basic` as a cost cap.** *Effort S. Benefit low, mostly hygiene, since `auto` is already the documented default. Risk low to medium.* Firecrawl's enhanced proxy is still a hosted escalation, and under *Reddit v. SerpApi* the customer who sets the parameters can be the circumventor. Document that, and let users pin `basic`. Treat Firecrawl's "no longer supported" response as `no_sanctioned_route`, not a retryable error.

8. **Add a news and paywall handler.** *Effort S–M. Benefit medium. Risk low.* For known bot-managed or paywalled publishers, return the search snippet, publisher RSS or Google News RSS item, the NYT Article Search abstract when a key is configured, and a clear "paywalled; open in browser" link. Do not run the generic chain against them. A later step could hand off to the user's own browser session (an extension or browser automation of a subscriber login). That hand-off keeps the fetch as the subscriber's own access, but it should never harvest cookies into the CLI.

9. **Support user-supplied keys for paid or approved APIs.** *Effort M. Benefit medium, narrow audience. Risk low within terms.* This covers X pay-per-use (about $5 per 1,000 post reads per third-party pricing), the Threads API for approved apps, and BYO Elsevier, Springer or Wiley TDM keys. Elsevier's TDM licence caps displayed snippets at 200 characters ([Elsevier TDM license](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/text-and-data-mining/license)), so the adapter must enforce that.

10. **Track Web Bot Auth signing and do not build it yet.** *Effort L. Benefit low today. Risk none.* Cloudflare and AWS WAF verify RFC 9421 signatures. Since 2026-07-01, Cloudflare's taxonomy distinguishes Direct from Intermediary signed agents, and its Verified-bot rules require robots compliance ([Cloudflare docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/concepts/bot/signed-agents/)). An individual CLI user would need a published key directory and registration that Cloudflare can reject without giving a reason, and the IETF webbotauth architecture draft's adoption is unconfirmed. Revisit if Cloudflare opens self-service registration for intermediary agents, or if hosted signers (Browserbase, Anchor) become an option search-cli can route through.

### Time-sensitive items and unverified claims

The calendar needs to be watched closely. Reddit's API intake closes **2026-10-31**, RSS ends **2026-11-13**, unregistered apps are cut off **2027-01-12**, and the public Data API closes in **March 2027**. These dates come via ppc.land's summary, not from the primary r/modnews posts, which were not fetched. Cloudflare has blocked "mixed-use" crawlers on ad-hosting pages by default since **2026-09-15** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/01/cloudflares-new-policy-pushes-ai-companies-to-pay-for-publishers-content/)). In *NYT v. OpenAI*, summary-judgment replies are reportedly due 2026-11-20 ([aivortex](https://www.aivortex.io/legal/ai-case-law/nyt-v-openai/)). An amended Google complaint against SerpApi is reported only by single secondary sources. The following claims are vendor-sourced or otherwise unverified: the `.json` shutdown date (scraper vendors), the Firecrawl enhanced-proxy pricing change, Firecrawl's per-platform blocks (competitor sources), the X per-read price and cap figures (third parties, conflicting), the Threads 500-queries-per-week limit, the April 2026 YouTube PO-token date (Apify page), the String benchmark (run by a ranked vendor), all Scrappey and ProxyLabs claims about impersonation efficacy, the Medium, Quora, Instagram, Facebook, Glassdoor and Discord login-wall details beyond our probe's status codes, and Telegram MTProto terms. Every legal statement relies on law-firm alerts and trade press rather than the orders themselves. None of this is legal advice.

## Conclusion

The research inverts the question search-cli was built around. The useful question is no longer how to get past the wall, because for social and publisher sites the wall now defines legal exposure as well as reliability. The useful question is which door the site left open. For most research targets one exists: an API, a feed, an open-access copy or a keyless JSON endpoint. Those doors are steadier than any fingerprint and carry far less risk. The sites with no door (LinkedIn, Quora, Glassdoor, Discord, Meta properties, and Reddit after November) are where the honest answer is a link and a reason, and a tool that says "blocked" is more useful to an agent than one that returns a login form as content.

The cheapest change has the largest effect. Content validation costs a day and stops the chain from treating walls as wins, and it is a prerequisite for measuring whether the stealth rung earns its place. The order of work should be validation, then routing, then retiring or gating the stealth rung on evidence; any newer impersonation profile should come after those, not before. The Reddit dates mean the routing work has a real deadline. The legal split between the two SerpApi courts means the impersonation question is unsettled law, and an open-source maintainer has no reason to volunteer as the test case.
