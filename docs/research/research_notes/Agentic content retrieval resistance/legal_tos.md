# Legal and ToS posture of automated web retrieval for an open-source agentic research CLI (US/EU, as of late Oct 2026)

Not legal advice. Most 2026 items below are sourced from law-firm alerts, trackers and trade press, not from the orders themselves; where only secondary sources exist this is flagged. Research date: 2026-10-31.

## Q1. US case law and status (CFAA, contract, scraping-specific cases)

### Takeaway
For logged-out access to public pages, US law has drifted steadily toward "not a CFAA problem, and often not a contract problem either" (Van Buren 2021, hiQ 2022, Meta v. Bright Data 2024, X v. Bright Data 2024, Ryanair v. Booking JMOL 2025). Risk concentrates in: (a) logged-in or fake-account scraping (hiQ lost on contract), (b) state-law claims that survive copyright preemption when they carry "extra elements" (Reddit v. Anthropic remand, 2026), and (c) the newer DMCA §1201 theory against defeating bot-detection (see Q2).

### Cited Findings
- **Van Buren v. United States (US Supreme Court, June 3, 2021)**: narrowed CFAA "exceeds authorized access" to a gates-up-or-down inquiry (whether you may enter a part of the system), not purpose-based use restrictions. — [Supreme Court opinion No. 19-783](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-783_k53l.pdf) (primary source, not fetched in this session; widely known holding)
- **hiQ v. LinkedIn**: Ninth Circuit (2022, on remand after Van Buren, 31 F.4th 1180) reaffirmed that scraping publicly available data likely is not "without authorization" under the CFAA. But in Nov 2022 the district court held hiQ's scraping and use of fake profiles breached LinkedIn's User Agreement; parties filed a stipulated consent judgment on Dec 6, 2022, entered Dec 8, 2022: $500,000 judgment against hiQ covering breach of contract, a CFAA violation and a California claim, plus a permanent injunction barring scraping and fake accounts. Settlement creates no binding precedent. — [Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/12/08/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case/); [LinkedIn newsroom](https://news.linkedin.com/2022/november/court-order-affirms-linkedin-s-legal-positions-against-hiq-in-da); [stipulation PDF](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/23429042/hiq-stip.pdf)
- **Meta v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal. 3:23-cv-00077-EMC, Jan 23, 2024, Judge Chen)**: Meta's terms govern "your use" of its products; Bright Data's logged-off scraping of public data was not "use" and so did not breach Meta's terms, nor did selling that data. — [Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/post/102kqw7/). (Meta later dropped the case, per general reporting; not verified in this session.)
- **X Corp v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal. 3:23-cv-03698-WHA, Judge Alsup)**: May 9, 2024 dismissal; the court held X's state-law contract/tort claims premised on scraping public content were preempted by the Copyright Act (X does not own users' content but granted itself only a non-exclusive license). X amended (Nov 26, 2024) to add CFAA, California CDAFA and DMCA claims; Bright Data counterclaimed on antitrust (Dec 17, 2024). One tracker lists the case as **stayed on a settlement in principle (verified May 18, 2026)**. No Ninth Circuit appeal found. — [FKKS "Blockbuster ruling"](https://ipandmedialaw.fkks.com/post/102j7d0/blockbuster-ruling-federal-court-holds-that-copyright-act-preempts-xs-web-scrap); [Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2024/05/14/california-court-issues-another-noteworthy-decision-dismissing-breach-of-contract-and-tort-claims-in-web-scraping-dispute/); [Goldman blog Jan 2025](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2025/01/catching-up-on-the-heavyweight-scraping-battle-between-x-and-bright-data-guest-blog-post.htm); [ailawsuittracker (settlement status, single source)](https://ailawsuittracker.com/cases/x-corp-v-bright-data-ltd-3-23-cv-03698-wha/)
- **Ryanair v. Booking.com (D. Del. 1:20-cv-01191-WCB)**: July 2024 jury found Booking liable under the CFAA for $5,000; Jan 2025 Judge Bryson granted JMOL overturning the verdict because Ryanair did not prove ≥$5,000 loss attributable to Booking (statutory prerequisite). Ryanair appealed (Feb 28, 2025). No appellate decision found as of this research. — [Goldman blog, Mar 2025](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2025/03/court-overturns-a-bad-jury-verdict-against-scraping-ryanair-v-booking-guest-blog-post.htm); [Cooley press release](https://www.cooley.com/news/coverage/2025/2025-01-22-cooley-secures-decisive-win-for-bookingcom-in-computer-fraud-and-abuse-act-case); [travelextra (appeal)](https://www.travelextra.ie/?p=67511)
- **Reddit v. Anthropic**: Judge Trina Thompson (N.D. Cal.) remanded to San Francisco Superior Court (order Mar 30/31, 2026; sources differ on date), holding the Copyright Act does not preempt Reddit's state-law claims (contract, unjust enrichment, etc.) because each has "extra elements". Now in state court (CGC-25-625892); Anthropic sought dismissal before discovery (July 2026, single-outlet report). — [Crowell & Moring](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/northern-district-of-california-court-holds-state-tort-and-contract-claims-not-preempted-by-federal-copyright-act-remands-reddit-v-anthropic-to-state-court); [Daily Journal](https://dailyjournal.com/article/390586-reddit-data-scraping-suit-against-anthropic-back-to-state-court); [Courthouse News](https://courthousenews.com/reddit-privacy-case-against-anthropic-kicked-back-to-state-court/); [ailawwiki](https://ailawwiki.com/News_Reddit_v_Anthropic_Remand_2026)
- **Note the tension**: X v. Bright Data (2024) found scraping-based state claims preempted; Reddit v. Anthropic (2026) found them not preempted. Same district, different judges and pleadings. — sources above
- **Dow Jones & NYP Holdings v. Perplexity (S.D.N.Y., filed Oct 2024)**: Judge Failla denied Perplexity's motion to dismiss/transfer (jurisdiction and venue only) in August 2025. No 2026 merits ruling found. — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/408413/dow-jones-faceoff-court-allows-case-against-perpl.html); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/perplexity-ai-cant-escape-dow-jones-training-copyright-suit)
- **Thomson Reuters v. Ross (3d Cir., Sept 29, 2026)**: affirmed Judge Bibas (Feb 11, 2025) that ROSS's copying of Westlaw headnotes for an AI legal-research tool was not fair use ("minimally transformative, at best"); headnotes sufficiently original. Court said reasoning does not reach generative AI. First federal appellate decision on AI-training fair use. Remanded for trial/damages. — [LawNext](https://www.lawnext.com/2026/09/3rd-circuit-rules-for-thomson-reuters-in-its-copyright-fight-against-legal-research-startup-ross.html); [Jackson Walker](https://www.jw.com/news/insights-ai-copyright-fair-use-case/); [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2026/10/01/understanding-the-reuters-ross-ai-ruling/)
- **Cloudflare v. Perplexity (not litigation, Aug 4, 2025)**: Cloudflare reported Perplexity used undeclared crawlers with a generic Chrome-on-macOS UA and IPs outside its published ranges to evade robots.txt disallows and WAF blocks on PerplexityBot/Perplexity-User; Cloudflare delisted Perplexity as a verified bot. In the same test ChatGPT-User fetched robots.txt and stopped when disallowed. — [Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/perplexity-is-using-stealth-undeclared-crawlers-to-evade-website-no-crawl-directives/)

### Inferences
- An individual user's CLI fetching a public, logged-out page is about as low-risk as US scraping law gets: no CFAA "gate" is passed (Van Buren/hiQ), and browsewrap terms are weak against logged-out actors (Meta v. Bright Data). Reddit v. Anthropic and the hiQ contract loss show the residual risk is contract/state-law, and it scales with commercial scale and with whether the actor ever agreed to terms (logged in, API key, account).
- Thomson Reuters v. Ross concerns building a competing product from copied content; it is weak authority against transient fetching for a user's reading/summarization, but argues against tools that persist/redistribute fetched content.

### Gaps
- **NYT v. OpenAI/Microsoft**: not researched in this session; I have no verified 2026 status. (From prior knowledge only: April 2025 S.D.N.Y. ruling let core copyright claims proceed; treat as unverified.)
- **Ryanair v. Booking** appeal outcome: none found.
- Whether Meta formally dismissed v. Bright Data: not verified here.
- **Reddit v. Anthropic** state-court demurrer outcome: not found.

## Q2. DMCA §1201 anti-circumvention applied to bot protection / paywalls (SearchGuard cases)

### Takeaway
This is the most important 2026 development. Two federal courts split in result on the same SearchGuard theory: N.D. Cal. (Google v. SerpApi, July 20, 2026) dismissed Google's §1201 claim (with prejudice where SearchGuard protects only uncopyrighted results; without prejudice otherwise), while S.D.N.Y. (Reddit v. SerpApi/Perplexity et al., July 31, 2026) let Reddit's §1201(a) claims proceed, holding a bot-detection system that admits humans but blocks bots is an "access control" even though the content is publicly viewable, and that the *user* of a circumvention tool (Perplexity) can be a direct circumventor. Defeating bot detection to reach copyrighted content is now a live DMCA exposure, not just a ToS issue.

### Cited Findings
- **Google v. SerpApi (N.D. Cal., Judge Gonzalez Rogers, July 20, 2026)**: dismissed with prejudice "to the extent [claims] are premised on instances where SearchGuard controls access to Google Search results that do not contain any copyrighted content"; dismissed without prejudice for results with copyrighted components because Google did not allege SearchGuard controls access "with the authority of the copyright owner". Court rejected SerpApi's argument that only copyright owners can sue (§1203 "any person injured") and did not accept that SerpApi's methods fall outside "circumvention". Leave to amend by Aug 10, 2026. — [ChatGPT Is Eating the World](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/07/20/judge-gonzalez-rogers-dismisses-googles-dmca-anti-circumvention-claim-v-serpapi-for-alleged-circumvention-of-searchguard-v-scraping-google-results/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/court-dismisses-googles-dmca-claims-against-serpapi/583033/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-serpapi-dmca-scraping-lawsuit-dismissed)
- A blog reports Google filed an amended complaint (~Aug 2026) narrowed to Knowledge Panels with licensed imagery — **unverified, single secondary source**. — [Beaumont & Sheridan blog](https://beaumontandsheridan.com/blog/google-serpapi-dmca-2026-07-28.html)
- **Reddit v. SerpApi, Oxylabs, AWMProxy, Perplexity (S.D.N.Y. 1:25-cv-08736, filed Oct 22, 2025; Judge Engelmayer, July 31, 2026)**: denied motions to dismiss on §1201(a)(1)(A) (circumvention) and §1201(a)(2) (trafficking) claims; dismissed §1201(b) claim (SearchGuard is an access control, not a rights-protection measure); dismissed unjust enrichment and unfair competition as copyright-preempted; civil conspiracy claim survived. Key reasoning: SearchGuard "effectively controls access" under §1201(a)(3)(B) though humans can view the same content, because it distinguishes automated from human users (analogy: facial recognition admitting residents only). Reddit plausibly authorized Google's measure (broad authorization suffices) and has standing via its own authored content plus lost licensing revenue and harm from bypassing deletions. Multi-sentence SERP snippets plausibly copyrightable. Perplexity plausibly a direct circumventor because it set parameters and ran the queries; SerpApi's tool allegedly used proxies, fake user-agent strings and "ludicrous speed" features. — [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/reddit-v-serpapi-llc); [CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/71720563/reddit-inc-v-serpapi-llc/); [Matthew Russell Lee substack](https://matthewrussellleeicp.substack.com/p/ai-beat-extra-in-reddit-case-against)
- Oxylabs/AWMProxy: one source says Oxylabs "stays in"; another says as of Sept 2026 Oxylabs' motion remained under submission. **Conflicting.** Reddit moved to dismiss SerpApi's counterclaims Sept 25, 2026. — [Matthew Russell Lee](https://matthewrussellleeicp.substack.com/p/ai-beat-extra-in-reddit-case-against); [Exhibit AI](https://exhibitai.info/cases/reddit-v-serpapi-oxylabs-uab-awmproxy-and-perplexity/)

### Inferences
- Reddit v. SerpApi's reasoning, if adopted elsewhere, reaches any bot-detection system (Cloudflare challenges, Akamai, DataDome, etc.) guarding copyrighted content: TLS/browser-fingerprint impersonation, UA spoofing and residential-proxy rotation designed to defeat it look like "circumvention", and shipping that capability in a tool could look like §1201(a)(2) trafficking. For an open-source project, the trafficking angle is the scariest one because it attaches to the tool's authors, not just its users. Open-source distribution does not obviously avoid §1201(a)(2) (cf. DeCSS cases), though no court has applied it to a general-purpose fetch library.
- Google v. SerpApi limits this where the protected content is uncopyrighted (facts, URLs, short snippets) or where the measure's operator lacks copyright-owner authorization. The law is unsettled across circuits; neither ruling is appellate.
- Paywalls/login walls are classic access controls; bypassing them is squarely §1201(a)(1)(A) territory independent of the SearchGuard debate.

### Gaps
- Primary orders not read in full; no appellate ruling on §1201 vs. bot detection exists as far as found.
- No case found applying §1201 to a plain HTTP client that merely sends a browser-like User-Agent with no other evasion.

## Q3. Contract/ToS: browsewrap vs clickwrap; logged-out vs logged-in

### Takeaway
Logged-out scrapers are hard to bind to browsewrap terms (Meta v. Bright Data; X v. Bright Data). Logged-in scrapers, account holders and API-key holders have assented (clickwrap) and face real contract liability (hiQ consent judgment). State-law claims may or may not be preempted by copyright depending on the court (X v. Bright Data vs. Reddit v. Anthropic).

### Cited Findings
- Meta's terms bind only "use" of the products; logged-off public scraping is not use — [FBM](https://www.fbm.com/post/102kqw7/)
- hiQ's fake-profile/logged-in scraping breached LinkedIn's User Agreement; $500k consent judgment and injunction — [Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/12/08/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case/)
- X's contract claims against logged-out scraping preempted (2024) — [FKKS](https://ipandmedialaw.fkks.com/post/102j7d0/blockbuster-ruling-federal-court-holds-that-copyright-act-preempts-xs-web-scrap)
- Reddit's contract/unjust enrichment claims against Anthropic not preempted (2026) — [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/northern-district-of-california-court-holds-state-tort-and-contract-claims-not-preempted-by-federal-copyright-act-remands-reddit-v-anthropic-to-state-court)
- In Reddit v. SerpApi, unjust enrichment/unfair competition were preempted but conspiracy predicated on DMCA survived — [Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/reddit-v-serpapi-llc)

### Inferences
- The CLI should never use the user's logged-in cookies/session to scrape a site whose terms forbid automated access; that converts a weak browsewrap position into a clickwrap breach by the user.
- Official APIs (approach 4) are contracts: using them within terms is low risk; using API keys outside terms (e.g. caching/redistribution prohibited by the API ToS) is a straightforward breach.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 appellate decision on browsewrap enforceability against scrapers found.

## Q4. EU: DSM Art. 4 TDM, opt-outs, AI Act GPAI Code, German rulings, GDPR

### Takeaway
EU TDM exception (DSM Directive Art. 4) permits reproductions for text and data mining unless rights are reserved "in an appropriate manner, such as machine-readable means"; OLG Hamburg (Dec 10, 2025) held natural-language ToS opt-outs are not machine-readable, favoring robots.txt-style signals; further appeal to the BGH admitted, and a CJEU referral (Like Company v. Google) is pending. The GPAI Code of Practice (July 2025) commits signatory model providers' crawlers to obey the Robots Exclusion Protocol. All of this is aimed at TDM/training, not per-request reading by a user; a user-directed fetch for reading/summarization is more naturally analyzed under temporary-copy (Art. 5 InfoSoc) and private use, which this session did not research.

### Cited Findings
- **Kneschke v. LAION**: LG Hamburg (Sept 27, 2024) held LAION's download covered by the scientific-research TDM exception (§60d UrhG); suggested natural-language ToS reservation might be machine-readable. **OLG Hamburg (5 U 104/24, Dec 10, 2025)** affirmed dismissal but held the natural-language opt-out did not satisfy machine-readability (must be "understandable" by machines; no specific format prescribed) and the wording did not expressly prohibit TDM. Appeal to the BGH admitted, so not final. — [CMS case tracker](https://cms.law/en/rou/publication/artificial-intelligence-and-copyright-case-tracker/germany-robert-kneschke-v-laion-e.v); [Freshfields](https://technologyquotient.freshfields.com/post/102mebl/ai-training-copyright-part-2-text-and-data-mining-under-court-scrutiny); [IPKat 2024](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2024/09/guest-post-german-court-finds-laions.html)
- CJEU referral on TDM (Like Company v. Google) is pending — [Freshfields](https://technologyquotient.freshfields.com/post/102mebl/ai-training-copyright-part-2-text-and-data-mining-under-court-scrutiny)
- **GPAI Code of Practice, copyright chapter (final, July 2025)**: signatories operating crawlers must use crawlers that read and follow Robot Exclusion Protocol instructions, also honor other machine-readable opt-outs adopted by standards bodies or widely adopted, and publish crawler details; search-engine operators must honor opt-outs without "adverse effects"; adherence does not by itself establish copyright compliance. REP chosen as the only international standard available. — [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/code-of-practice-for-general-purpose-ai-models-published-compliance-just-got-clearer-participation-still-optional); [Communia](https://communia-association.org/2025/07/21/our-thoughts-on-the-final-version-of-the-gpai-code-of-practice/); [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/eu-artificial-intelligence-office-publishes-the-final-version-of-the-gpai-code-of-practice)
- DSM Directive Art. 4(3) text (reservation "in an appropriate manner, such as machine-readable means in the case of content made publicly available online") — [EUR-Lex 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/790/oj) (primary, not fetched this session)

### Inferences
- The Code binds GPAI model providers that signed it, not a third-party open-source CLI; but it sets the industry norm "crawlers obey robots.txt", which courts may cite.
- A CLI that only fetches pages a user asked for and does not build a corpus is outside the training/TDM frame; if the project ever caches or aggregates content for mining, Art. 4 opt-outs (robots.txt, TDMRep `tdm-reservation`) become relevant and should be honored.

### Gaps
- **GDPR** for personal data in scraped content: not researched in this session. (From prior knowledge only, unverified: Dutch AP 2024 guidance took a very restrictive view of scraping by private parties; CNIL 2025 guidance accepts legitimate interest for AI scraping with safeguards; Clearview fines.) For a CLI that fetches and summarizes transiently on a user's behalf, the household exemption may apply to the individual user, but the project/hosted service would not have it.
- **GEMA v. OpenAI (LG Munich, Nov 2025)** and any Dutch rulings: not researched.
- TDMRep (W3C CG) legal status beyond "widely adopted machine-readable means": no source gathered.
- BGH status of LAION appeal as of Oct 2026: not checked.

## Q5. Is robots.txt binding? User-initiated fetchers vs crawlers

### Takeaway
robots.txt (RFC 9309, 2022) is a voluntary protocol, not an access control; ignoring it is not itself unlawful in the US, though it can be evidence of bad faith and, in the EU, a valid machine-readable TDM opt-out. Major vendors split it: training/search crawlers obey robots; user-triggered fetchers are documented as possibly not obeying it (OpenAI ChatGPT-User, Perplexity-User), but evading blocks with disguised agents (Cloudflare's Perplexity finding) drew public sanction.

### Cited Findings
- RFC 9309 states the rules "are not a form of access authorization" — [RFC 9309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309) (primary; text not fetched this session)
- OpenAI: "ChatGPT-User is not used for crawling the web in an automatic fashion." "Because these actions are initiated by a user, robots.txt rules may not apply." Disallowing GPTBot signals no training use; sites opted out of OAI-SearchBot are not shown in ChatGPT search answers. — [OpenAI bots docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)
- Perplexity documents Perplexity-User as generally ignoring robots.txt since it fetches on user request (secondary report of vendor docs). — [honeyb.ai reference](https://www.honeyb.ai/blog/ai-crawler-user-agents-reference-2026); [Rueseo](https://ruesandora.com/writing/user-triggered-ai-bots)
- Anthropic Claude-User robots.txt handling: secondary sources conflict (one says it follows robots.txt; others say not stated). Check [Anthropic support article](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518). — [anagram.ai](https://www.anagram.ai/blog/ai-crawlers-explained-gptbot-claudebot-perplexitybot-and-how-to-let-them-in-2026)
- Cloudflare test: ChatGPT-User fetched robots.txt and stopped when disallowed (in practice more conservative than its docs); Perplexity rotated undeclared IPs and a Chrome UA after blocks — [Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/perplexity-is-using-stealth-undeclared-crawlers-to-evade-website-no-crawl-directives/)

### Inferences
- Defensible norm for a CLI: honor robots.txt for any automated multi-page/crawl or background behavior; for a single URL the user explicitly supplies, fetching despite robots disallow is industry-standard (OpenAI/Perplexity) but should use an honest, distinct UA so sites can block it. The legally dangerous step is not ignoring robots.txt; it is *disguising identity after being blocked*, which in light of Reddit v. SerpApi can be framed as circumvention.

### Gaps
- No court decision found holding robots.txt legally binding per se in US; EU treatment only via TDM opt-out.

## Q6. Per-approach risk posture (synthesis with citations)

### Takeaway
Ranked lowest to highest risk: (4) official APIs within terms < (1) direct public fetch with honest UA < (7) Wayback < (5) unofficial public JSON endpoints < (9) ignoring robots for user-initiated single fetches (low legal, moderate reputational) < (8) hosted providers (risk shifts partly to provider, but they are active defendants) < (6) alternate frontends < (7b) archive.today < (2) fingerprint impersonation to evade bot detection < (3) login/paywall circumvention.

### Cited Findings
- (1) Direct public fetch: CFAA unlikely (Van Buren; hiQ), browsewrap weak (Meta v. Bright Data) — [FBM](https://www.fbm.com/post/102kqw7/); [Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/12/08/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case/)
- (2) TLS/browser fingerprint impersonation: Reddit v. SerpApi treats bot-detection as a §1201(a) access control and proxies/fake UAs as circumvention; tool makers face trafficking claims, users direct-circumvention claims — [Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/reddit-v-serpapi-llc). Counterweight: Google v. SerpApi dismissal where protected content uncopyrighted — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/court-dismisses-googles-dmca-claims-against-serpapi/583033/)
- (3) Login/paywall circumvention: logged-in scraping → contract breach (hiQ); paywalls are access controls (§1201(a)) — [Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/12/08/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case/); [Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/reddit-v-serpapi-llc)
- (6) Alternate frontends: YouTube sent Invidious a cease-and-desist (June 2023) alleging YouTube API ToS violations, 7-day takedown; Invidious refused, arguing it never used or agreed to the API terms (it scrapes the website). No lawsuit found. — [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/youtube-tells-open-source-privacy-software-invidious-to-shut-down); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2023/6/youtube-legal-team-asked-invidious-developers-to-take-down-the-service-within-7-days)
- (7) archive.today: FBI subpoena to registrar Tucows dated Oct 30, 2025 seeking registrant identity in a federal criminal investigation (authenticity not officially confirmed); English Wikipedia reportedly blacklisting archive.today as of Feb 22, 2026; single-source report of injected JavaScript on its CAPTCHA page from ~Jan 11, 2026 (unverified). — [giga.law](https://giga.law/daily-news/2025/11/6/fbi-subpoena-seeks-information-on-registrant-of-archive-domains); [factually.co](https://factually.co/fact-checks/media/archive-today-fbi-subpoena-authenticity-verified-prank-possibility-fe82a4); [texxr timeline](https://texxr.com/entity/archive.today); [f3ds blog (unverified)](https://f3ds.vercel.app/posts/archive-today-ddos-russian-mystery)
- (8) Hosted providers: Bright Data won against Meta and X (2024); Oxylabs, AWMProxy and SerpApi are defendants in Reddit's DMCA suit, and Perplexity (the customer) is alleged a direct circumventor for using SerpApi — [Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/reddit-v-serpapi-llc); [Exhibit AI](https://exhibitai.info/cases/reddit-v-serpapi-oxylabs-uab-awmproxy-and-perplexity/)
- (9) robots.txt for user-initiated fetches: see Q5 — [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots); [Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/perplexity-is-using-stealth-undeclared-crawlers-to-evade-website-no-crawl-directives/)

### Inferences
- **Hosted unblockers**: using one does not launder liability; Reddit v. SerpApi holds the customer that sets parameters and runs queries can be the direct circumventor. Providers that merely fetch/render (e.g. Jina Reader, Firecrawl in basic mode) differ from those marketing "unblocking" (residential proxies, CAPTCHA solving); the latter marketing is exactly the "designed or marketed for circumvention" element. Reading each provider's ToS (most push compliance responsibility onto the customer) was not done here.
- **Unofficial JSON endpoints** (e.g. Reddit's `.json`): public, logged-out, no circumvention, so mostly ToS/browsewrap risk; but Reddit is the most litigious platform on scraping in 2025-26, so heavy or commercial use is risky; light per-user use is low risk.
- **Wayback Machine**: fetching an existing snapshot is reading a third-party archive; Internet Archive's own legal exposure (Hachette, music labels) does not attach to readers. archive.today is reputationally and operationally risky (paywall-defeating purpose, criminal investigation, Wikipedia blacklist); a tool defaulting to it to bypass paywalls inherits the paywall-circumvention framing.
- **Recommended posture for the open-source CLI** (consistent with all of the above): identify honestly with a distinct UA and contact URL; fetch only user-requested URLs; respect robots.txt for any automated/background/multi-page crawling and offer it as default-on for single fetches with an explicit override; never retry with spoofed identity after a block/challenge; do not ship TLS-fingerprint impersonation or CAPTCHA-solving aimed at bot detection (trafficking risk for maintainers after Reddit v. SerpApi); no login/paywall bypass and no use of the user's session cookies for scraping; prefer official APIs; treat hosted unblocking providers as opt-in user configuration with clear disclaimer; avoid persistent caching/redistribution of full content (copyright/Ross; EU TDM opt-outs).

### Gaps
- Nitter and Redlib: no legal action found in this session (Nitter's historical breakage came from X API/guest-token changes, not litigation — unverified).
- Firecrawl, Zyte, Jina: no litigation or ToS analysis gathered.
- Whether §1201 trafficking could reach an open-source library offering TLS impersonation (e.g. curl_cffi-style) has no direct precedent found.
